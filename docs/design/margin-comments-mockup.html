Margin comments mock-up

Ways to comment on a highlighted passage, the way Google Docs does it, and to give section comments the same treatment, so opening a thread never shoves the page down. Three layouts follow, A, B and C, each drawn whole with passage and section threads together; what works the same in all three comes after them. Nothing here is wired up.

Recommendation

Layout C, the side panel folded to a rail, for passage and section threads alike, with Subtle highlights. Section threads are long agent conversations: in A an open one pushes every card below it off its line, and in B it covers the text, while C gives it the panel’s full height and costs 2.75rem when folded. If a phone layout is wanted now, P2, the bottom sheet.

Layout A: cards in the margin

A card per thread in a 17rem margin, level with what it is about. A passage card shows its quote; a section card sits level with the section’s chip and is labelled Section. A folded card shows the first message and the reply count; the open card widens toward the text, joined to it by a lavender tick; cards below it move down rather than overlap.

  • Every thread is visible beside its words, with its status, without a click.
  • A section thread is just one more card; the chip opens it in place.
  • Needs a 17rem margin all the time.
  • One long open thread, usually a section thread, pushes every card below it off its line.

A wide-screen layout: scroll the frame sideways to see the margin.

Lotuspod:Reply checks on open pages

How a page checks for replies

While a comment waits, the page checks every five seconds for a reply. Checks run only while some thread on the page is still waiting, so a page left open overnight costs nothing once its threads are answered.

Each check is answered from the server's cache: it asks only for threads that changed since the last check, and an empty answer is a few bytes, so a dozen open tabs barely register.

In a background tab the page slows to one check a minute and catches up the moment the tab comes back, so a reader who switches away misses nothing.

What a check costs

An empty check returns about 200 bytes and takes under 10 ms on the server, so even a page left open all week adds little load.

Layout B: markers in the margin

The margin holds only a small marker per thread, level with its passage: who is in it, how many messages, and a dot while an agent is writing. A section thread gets a § marker level with its chip. Clicking a marker or the chip opens the thread in a popover anchored to the marker, over the text.

  • The page stays nearly as clean as today; the gutter costs about 4rem.
  • Section and passage threads look the same: one marker each.
  • Nothing to read until you click; status is only a dot.
  • The popover covers the text beside it, and a long section thread makes a tall popover.

Layout C: side panel

A fixed panel on the right lists every thread in page order, grouped under its section heading: passage threads by number, the section's own thread marked §. Clicking a chip, a highlight or a number opens the panel and scrolls it to that thread; hovering a thread in the panel lights its passage.

  • Every thread in one list, in page order, grouped by section: easy to review.
  • Long threads, section threads above all, get the panel’s full height and scroll on their own.
  • Folds to a 2.75rem rail that still shows each thread’s state.
  • 20rem of width while open.
  • The tie to a passage is a number and a hover, not a position.

Highlight styles

The persistent highlight on commented text, in two strengths. Hover and the open thread are drawn on the same sentence; the last rows show two threads that overlap and two that touch. Hover a highlight here to see it live.

Where two threads cover the same words the tints stack, so the shared part reads darker; hovering or opening one lights only its own range. Touching highlights keep a hairline gap and their own rounded ends. Subtle keeps a page with many comments readable; Strong is easier to spot on a long page but turns the open passage into a solid block.

Phone

Lower priority: the maintainer reads mostly on desktop. Each frame is 360px wide. On a phone there is no margin, so a thread opens from its highlight or chip; P2's sheet holds section threads too.
